# Long delays and compensation

The Regulation's text only mentions compensation for cancellation and denied boarding. The Court of Justice of the EU (Sturgeon, joined cases C-402/07 and C-432/07) decided that passengers whose flight **arrives three hours or more late** at the final destination have the same right to compensation as passengers of a cancelled flight.
The delay is measured at arrival, when at least one aircraft door is opened (Germanwings, C-452/13), not at departure.
A delay of 2 hours 59 minutes does not qualify. A delay of exactly 3 hours does.
The airline does not have to pay if it proves the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances that could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken.
