# Extraordinary circumstances (Article 5(3))

The airline is exempt from paying compensation if the disruption was caused by extraordinary circumstances which could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken.

Usually accepted as extraordinary: severe weather that makes operation unsafe, air-traffic-control restrictions and ATC strikes, security risks, political instability, bird strikes, and a medical emergency on board.

Usually **not** extraordinary:
- technical problems found during routine maintenance or arising from normal operation (Wallentin-Hermann, C-549/07);
- a strike by the airline's own staff, such as pilots or cabin crew (Airhelp v SAS, C-28/20);
- delays caused by a late incoming aircraft for ordinary operational reasons;
- overbooking.

Airlines often cite extraordinary circumstances that do not hold up, so a disputed reason should be reviewed by a person rather than accepted automatically.
